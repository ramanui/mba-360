Accreditation

/əˌkrɛdɪˈteɪʃən/ noun Last updated 20 July 2026

Accreditation is a formal process used to evaluate whether an organisation, institution, programme or service meets a set of predefined quality standards. It is carried out by an independent accrediting body that assesses performance against established criteria before granting official recognition.

Rather than being a one-time approval, accreditation is a continuous quality assurance mechanism. Accredited organisations are expected to maintain standards over time and often undergo periodic reviews to demonstrate that they continue to meet the required benchmarks.

What does accreditation mean?

The word accreditation refers to the act of officially recognising that an organisation or programme satisfies recognised standards of quality and competence. It serves as an assurance that the accredited entity has been evaluated through a structured and impartial process.

Accreditation is widely used across different industries, including:

Education Healthcare Laboratories Certification bodies Testing & inspection Engineering Manufacturing Professional training Environmental management

Although the evaluation criteria vary between sectors, the underlying purpose remains the same — ensuring quality, consistency and reliability.

Why is accreditation important?

Accreditation builds confidence among stakeholders by demonstrating that recognised standards have been met. It promotes accountability and encourages organisations to maintain high levels of performance instead of relying solely on self-declared quality claims.

Some of the key purposes of accreditation include:

  • Establishing recognised quality standards.
  • Promoting transparency and accountability.
  • Encouraging continuous improvement.
  • Increasing public trust and confidence.
  • Supporting consistency in processes and outcomes.
  • Providing independent verification of quality.
  • Helping organisations benchmark their performance.

Accreditation also creates a common framework that allows organisations to measure themselves against accepted national or international standards.

Did you know?

Accreditation is generally voluntary in many sectors. Organisations often choose to become accredited because it demonstrates their commitment to quality, even when it is not legally required. However, certain industries or professional programmes may mandate accreditation through regulatory requirements.

How does the accreditation process work?

While the exact procedure differs across industries, most accreditation systems follow a similar sequence of evaluation. The process is designed to be objective, evidence-based and independent. A typical accreditation process includes:

  • Submission of an application.
  • Self-assessment against established standards.
  • Review of policies, procedures and documentation.
  • On-site assessment or external evaluation.
  • Identification of strengths and improvement areas.
  • Final accreditation decision.
  • Periodic surveillance or renewal assessments.

Accreditation is usually granted for a defined period rather than permanently. Organisations must continue to meet the required standards to retain their accredited status.

Types of accreditation

Accreditation can be applied in different ways depending on what is being evaluated. The scope may cover an entire organisation, a specific programme or even a particular service.

01 · INSTITUTIONAL

Institutional accreditation

Evaluates an entire organisation by examining governance, quality management, infrastructure, leadership, resources and overall performance.

02 · PROGRAMME

Programme accreditation

Focuses on a specific programme, course or service instead of the entire organisation. The evaluation is limited to the standards applicable to that programme.

03 · PROFESSIONAL

Professional accreditation

Assesses whether a programme prepares individuals according to the standards expected within a particular profession or industry.

Each type serves a different purpose, but all share the common objective of verifying quality through independent assessment.

What are the benefits of accreditation?

Accreditation benefits organisations as well as the people who interact with them. It provides a recognised framework for maintaining quality and encourages continuous development. Some of its major benefits are:

  • Demonstrates commitment to recognised standards.
  • Enhances organisational credibility.
  • Builds confidence among stakeholders.
  • Encourages continuous improvement.
  • Improves consistency in operations.
  • Supports better governance and quality management.
  • Promotes accountability and transparency.
  • Helps identify opportunities for improvement.

Accreditation can also encourage organisations to adopt best practices and regularly review their systems to remain aligned with evolving standards.

Fun fact

The word “accredit” comes from the Latin credere, meaning “to believe” or “to trust.” This o reflects the primary purpose of accreditation — to establish trust by confirming an organisation or programme has been independently assessed ag recognised standards.

Common misconceptions

Accreditation is often misunderstood because it is confused with several other forms of recognition. Understanding these differences helps explain its actual purpose.

Accreditation is the same as certification.

Certification generally applies to individuals, products or management systems, whereas accreditation recognises the compet organisations or programmes to perform specific activities.

Accreditation is permanent.

Most accreditation statuses are valid only for a specific period and require renewal after periodic evaluations.

Accreditation guarantees perfection.

Accreditation confirms compliance with recognised standards but does not imply that an organisation is flawless or superior in every aspect.

Every organisation must be accredited.

Many organisations operate without accreditation. Whether it is required depends on the industry, regulations and organisational objectives.

Accreditation should therefore be viewed as an independent measure of quality rather than a guarantee of excellence in every situation.

In summary

Accreditation is an internationally recognised method of assessing quality, competence and compliance with established standards. By relying on independent evaluation instead of self-declaration, it creates confidence, encourages accountability and promotes continuous improvement across a wide range of industries. Whether applied to an institution, programme or service, it remains one of the most trusted mechanisms for demonstrating that recognised quality standards have been achieved and maintained.
